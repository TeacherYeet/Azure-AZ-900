Review: Cloud Concepts

This is a quick-reference review, not a full re-teach. If any of these terms feel unfamiliar, go back to the Assignment 1 lessons before continuing. Everything here maps directly to Microsoft's "Describe cloud concepts" exam domain.

Cloud computing, in one line

Cloud computing is a model for enabling on-demand access to a shared pool of configurable computing resources — servers, networking, storage, applications and services — that are ubiquitous, convenient, and can be rapidly provisioned or released with minimal management effort or provider interaction. It delivers faster innovation, flexible resources, and economies of scale.

Shared responsibility model

Security and management responsibilities are split between the cloud provider and the customer. How much the provider takes on depends on the service type:

Service typeWho manages what
On-premisesCustomer manages everything, top to bottom.
IaaSProvider manages the physical infrastructure; customer manages OS, apps, and data.
PaaSProvider also manages the OS and runtime; customer focuses on apps and data.
SaaSProvider manages almost everything; customer mainly manages access and data.

Cloud deployment models

ModelCharacteristics
Private cloudBuilt in an organization's own datacenter. The organization is responsible for operating it and does not provide access to outside users.
Public cloudOwned by a cloud provider (e.g. Microsoft), providing resources to multiple organizations over the internet.
Hybrid cloudCombines public and private cloud so applications can run in the most appropriate location.

CapEx vs. OpEx

Capital Expenditure (CapEx)Operational Expenditure (OpEx)
When you payUp-front, before useAs you go, billed immediately
What it coversPhysical infrastructure you buy and ownServices or products consumed on demand
Value over timeReduces over time (depreciation)No upfront cost — you only pay for what you use

Cloud providers operate on a consumption-based model: you pay only for the resources you actually use. Benefits include no upfront costs, better cost prediction, and the ability to start or stop paying for a resource as your needs change.

Benefits of the cloud

  • High availability — the ability to keep a service running, even through disruptions.
  • Scalability — adding or removing resources to match demand, manually or automatically.
  • Elasticity — automatically scaling resources to match usage in real time.
  • Reliability — a system's ability to recover from failures and keep functioning.
  • Predictability — consistent performance and predictable costs, through monitoring and forecasting.
  • Security — protection of data and infrastructure from threats, built into the platform.
  • Governance — applying policies and standards consistently, at scale.
  • Manageability — configuring, monitoring, and automating resources both in the cloud and on-premises.
💡 Exam tip
Scalability and elasticity are easy to mix up. Scalability is the general ability to scale up/out; elasticity specifically means the system scales automatically in response to demand.
