Exam Day: What to Expect

Once you feel ready, here is what the real AZ-900 exam looks like in practice — format, timing, scoring, and how to book it — plus some practical tips for the day itself.

Exam format

AspectWhat to expect
Number of questionsRoughly 40–60 questions.
Time allowedApproximately 60 minutes (plus time for sign-in instructions and a short survey).
Question formatsMultiple choice (one correct answer), multiple answer (select all that apply), drag-and-drop / matching, and short case studies.
Passing score700 out of 1000.
Where to take itOnline, proctored remotely from home or another quiet location, or in person at a testing center — both delivered through Pearson VUE.
CostThe exam has a fee. Check the current price on the Microsoft Learn / Pearson VUE site, as pricing varies by region.
💡 Booking the exam
AZ-900 is scheduled through Pearson VUE via the official Microsoft Learn certification page. You will need a Microsoft account, a valid photo ID, and — for the online-proctored option — a quiet room, a working webcam, and a stable internet connection.

Practical tips

  1. Read every question carefully. Watch for keywords like NOT, MOST, LEAST, BEST, or FIRST — they change which answer is correct, and it's easy to miss them under time pressure.
  2. Use flag-and-return. If a question stumps you, flag it and move on. Answer everything you're confident about first, then come back to the flagged questions with the time you have left.
  3. Don't overthink it. AZ-900 is a fundamentals-level exam. It tests whether you understand core concepts, not deep technical implementation details. If an answer feels overly complicated, it's usually not the one being asked for.
  4. Eliminate obviously wrong answers first. Even when you're unsure of the exact right answer, ruling out one or two clearly incorrect options greatly improves your odds.
  5. Manage your time. With roughly 40–60 questions in about 60 minutes, aim to spend no more than a minute or so per question on your first pass.
  6. Answer every question. There is no penalty for guessing, so never leave a question blank — even an educated guess has a chance of being right.
  7. Get a good night's sleep beforehand. Fatigue affects reading comprehension far more than most people expect, especially on a keyword-sensitive exam like this one.
