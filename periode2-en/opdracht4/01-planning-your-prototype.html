Planning Your Prototype

Before you touch Azure, you write a short proposal. A good proposal forces you to think through what you are actually going to build — so that when you start clicking around in the Azure portal, you already know what "done" looks like.

What makes a good technical proposal?

A proposal is not a essay — it is a working plan. A good proposal for this assignment is small, concrete and testable. "I will build something with Azure" is not a plan; "I will host a static portfolio website with three pages on an Azure Storage Account, using its static website feature" is a plan. Keep these things in mind:

  • Small scope. One page or a handful of pages is plenty. You can always extend it later (see the extra challenge).
  • Concrete functionality. Describe what a visitor can actually do or see, not just "a website".
  • Named services. Say exactly which Azure service you will use (e.g. Azure Storage static website hosting, or Azure App Service Free F1 tier) — not just "the cloud".
  • A clear definition of done. Decide in advance how you will know your prototype works.
💡 Why this matters
In a real job, a proposal is what you show your supervisor or client before you start building. Discussing your plan first — and getting agreement on scope — saves you from building the wrong thing. Treat your teacher as your "opdrachtgever" (client) for this assignment: if your plan changes significantly while building, discuss it with them first.
